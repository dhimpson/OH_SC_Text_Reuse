Whereas, under the Act of the General Assembly, approved February 3, 1870, a public ferry was established from a place known as Dill’s Bluff, on James Island, to the city of Charleston, and the same vested in Toney Stafford, his heirs and assigns, for the term of fourteen years, and Eugene V. Toomer is now the sole and lawful assignee thereof, and entitled to and all the rights formerly granted to said Toney Statfor doubts have arisen whether said charter has been affected by mis-user or non-user, and the public convenience requires a regular ferry between the aforesaid points Section 1. Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the State of South Carolina, now met and sitting in General Assembly, and by the authority of the same, That any and all the rights in and to said ferry heretofore granted to said Toney Stafford be, and now are, recognized and confirmed | unto the said Hugene V. Toomer, his heirs and assigns, for the unexpired term of said charter. Sec. 2. That from and after such unexpired term, the said Eugene V. Toomer, his heirs and assigns, shall enjoy the exclusive charter of said ferry for the term of fourteen years, with the right to charge and collect ferriage at the rates heretofore existing in regard to said ferry: Provided, That the said ferry be fully established and in good working order within thirty days after the passage of this Act.
