AN ACT IN RELATION TO THE CHURCH HOME INCORPORATED BY ACT OF THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY PASSED 16TH DAY OF DECEMBER, ANNO DOMINI 1852, AND TO CONFIRM THE ACTS OF THE COUNCIL OF THE SAME.
Whereas by an Act of the General Assembly passed on the 16th day of December, Anno Domini 1852, Cranmore Wallace, Paul T. Keith, William Dehon, William B. W. Howe, Edward Frost, WilPreamble. liam Aiken, J. M. Campbell, Edward B. White, Charles D. Carr, William Patton and Jacob K. Sass, then members of the Council of the Church Home, an institution connected with the Protestant Episcopal Church in this State for the support of destitute females and orphan girls, and their successors in office, were declared and constituted a body politic and corporate, by the name of the Church Home; and whereas but two of the said Council are now living and resident in this State, and irregularities have occurred in the appointment and election of their successors for the continuance and confirmation of the said institution, and of the rights, powers and privileges conferred in and by the said Act of incorporation: Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the State of South Carolina, now met and sitting in General Assembly, and by the authority of the same, That William B. W. Howe, Successors of Charles Coatesworth Pinckney, A. Toomer Porter, John Johnson,William H. Hanckel, W. O. Prentiss, Edward L. Kerrison, E. W. Edgerton, Henry E. Young, John Hanckel, William C. Courtney, J. J. Pringle Smith, Thomas M. Hanckel, William C. Bee and William McBurney, the present members of the said Council of the said Church Home, are hereby recognized and confirmed as successors of the above named original corporators, and invested with all the rights, powers and privileges of said original corporators, Acts heretoand that all acts done by them, or by any persons heretofore acting as successors of said original corporators in the corporate capacity as Council of the said Church Home, be, and the same are hereby, confirmed and declared as good and valid as if there had been no irregularities in the appointment of such successors.
