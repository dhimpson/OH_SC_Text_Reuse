AN ACT TO ENABLE THE SINKING FUND COMMISSION TO LEND FUNDS TO THE COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS OF ANDERSON COUNTY TO BUILD THE COURT HOUSE AND
JAIL IN SAID COUNTY, AND TO AUTHORIZE THE SAID COUNTY COMMISSIONERS TO PLEDGE THE SPECIAL TAX LEVY TO SECURE THE SAME.
SECTION 1. Be it enacted by the General Assembly of the Sinking Fund State of South Carolina, That the Sinking Fund Commislend funds to sion of the State of South Carolina are hereby authorized ty for court to lend to the County Board of Commissioners of Anderson County, out of the funds in their hands, the sum of twenty-eight thousand dollars, to be used to build the court house and jail for said county.
SECTION 2. That said loan shall be for the space of five how to be paid, years, and shall bear interest at a rate not exceeding five & c. per cent. per annum. And the County Board of Commissioners shall pay the said loan out of the taxes authorized to be levied and collected for the purpose of building said court house and jail by an Act entitled " An Act to enable the County Board of Commissioners of Anderson County to build a court house and jail for said county, & c., " approved the 17th of February, 1897, in the following manner: That is to say, they shall pay seven thousand dollars of the principal and interest each year for the space of four years, and the fifth year they shall pay such balance of the principal and interest as shall remain unpaid.
SECTION 3. That the Treasurer and Supervisor of said County of Anderson are hereby authorized to execute a note cuted. or notes to the said Sinking Fund Commission for the amount of said loan, and the special tax of one mill authorized to be levied and collected by the Act hereinbefore referred to, approved February 17th, 1897, shall be pledged by the County Board of Commissioners to secure the payment of the said note or notes.
SECTION 4. This Act shall go into effect immediately upon its approval.
SECTION 5. That all Acts and parts of Acts inconsistent with this Act be, and the same are hereby, repealed.
