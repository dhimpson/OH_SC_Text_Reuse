Sec. 2. That a tax not to exceed three mills upon every dollar of the value of all taxable property in each of the Counties of this State, except the Counties of Lancaster and Georgetown, which shall be four mills, and in the County of Aiken, which shall be three mills, be, and the same is hereby, levied for County purposes for the fiscal year commencing November 1, 1878, except that in the following named Counties the additional tax shall be, and the same is hereby, levied in the manner and for the purposes herein named, that is to say: In the County of Abbeville, one-half of one mill for past indebtedness and three-fourths of one mill to be levied for public fence; in the County of Aiken, two and one-half mills, of which one and one-half mills shall be applied to the payment of the past due indebtedness of the County and one mill to the payment. Of past due school claims. That there be levied upon the taxable property of Charleston County an additional tax of three fourths of one mill, to be collected by the County Treasurer at the same time when the other taxes are collected, the proceeds whereof shall be appropriated as follows: The one-third thereof, if so much be necessary, to the amount required under the provisions of the Act entitled “An Act to provide for funding the debt of Charleston County, &c.,” and the other two-thirds to be appropriated to the payment of the deficiencies in the said County for the fiscal years 1877-78: Provided, That before any such deficiency be paid the claim therefor must be established before the Board of County Commissioners: Provided, also, That in case the amount so provided for this purpose does not equal said deficiency, the claims so established shall be paid pro rata. That the County Commissioners of Charleston County be, and they are heréby, authorized and required to levy a tax from all the taxable property of the said County sufficient to raise the sum of three thousand dollars, which tax shall be paid at the same time with other tuxes. The proceeds arising therefrom shall be held by the Treasurer of the said County subject to the draft of the Major General commanding the First Division, and shall by him be apportioned as follows: The one-tenth thereof to the use of the militia in said County without the city of Charleston; another tenth thereof to the use of the First Regiment of the National Guard, and the remaining eight-tenths to be equaily divided amovg the companies composing the Fourth Brigade, by companies. That the City Treasurer of the city of Charleston shall levy and collect, when the first installment of city taxes are paid, an additional tax of one-quarter of one mill on the dollar on all the taxable property in the said city and apply the proceeds of the said tax to the payment of the deficiencies for the fiscal year 18771878 of the school fund of the school district composed of the city of Charleston; and that the Board of School Commissioners of the city of Charleston account to the City Treasurer of the said city for the value of all bills of the Bank of the State apportioned to said Board from the amount received tendered in payment of taxes of the said fiscal year, first paying in full any sum remaining unpaid as such deficiency; and in the County of Charleston one mill of the three mill tax levied for County purposes shall be set apart and kept separate by the County Treasurer for the payment of Court expenses, to wit: Dieting prisoners, pay of jurors, Constables in attendance on the Court, witnesess in State cases, Clerk of Court and Sheriffs’ accounts; and the County Treasurer, in each of his reports to the County Commissioners of funds on hand, shall specify the amount in his hands for Court expenses, and the County Commissioners are hereby prohibited from drawing checks on the fund so created for any other purpose; the County Commissioners of Charleston County are hereby authorized and required to pay from any funds collected for the fiscal year 1878 any sums that remain due for making assessments for the fiscal year 1876; in the County of Richland, one mill for past indebtedness of the County, as provided by Act passed at the regular session of 1874-75; in the County of Chester, four-fifths of one mill for past due school claims of the County, the proceeds of which, with the school funds now in the County Treasury, shall be applied to the payment of the Commissioners for investigating school claims and to the satisfaction of the claims approved by them in the manner and to the amount proposed in their report made to the General Assembly; in the County of Chesterfield, three mills for the past indebtedness of the County, in accordance with a Joint Resolution p at this session; in the County of Clarendon, one mill for past indebtedness; in the County of Darlington, three mills for past indebtedness of the County; for the County of Colleton, one mill for deficiencies of 1877-1878; in the County of Edgefield, one-half of one mill for current expenses and one mill for deficiencies for the year 1877-78; in the County of Fairfield, one and one-half mills to build public fence; in the County of Hampton, two mills for building the Court house and jail provided for at the last session of the General Assembly; in the County of Kershaw, two mills for past indebtedness of the County; in the County of Lancaster, two mills for past indebtedness of said County, to be paid in such manner and upon such terms as the County Commissioners and the several creditors may agree; in the townships of Indian Land, Waxhaw and Cane Creek, together with that portion of Gill’s Creek township adjoining Cane Creek and lying West of the Concord road, in Lancaster County, six mills, if so much be necessary, to be applied to the payment of the expenses of erecting and mainotain‘ng the fences directed to be established; in the County of Laurens, one and one-fifth mills for the past indebtedness of said County as ascertained by the Commission to inquire into the actual bona fide indebtedness of said County and one mill for deficiencies of the County for the past fiscal year, one-half mill for building County fence; in the County of Marion, two mills for past indebtedness of the County, if so much be necessary, in accordance with an Act approved December 24th, 1875, to be applied first to payment of indebtedness prior to passage of said Act, and should any balance remain, to be applied to any past indebtedness in said County; in the County of Marlboro, two mills to pay the past indebtedness of Marlboro County found to be bona fide by a Commission appointed under an Act to ascertain the actual bona fide indebtedness of the various Counties in this State and to regulate the payment of the same, approved June 11, 1877, also one and a fourth mills to pay deficiencies for fiscal year ending October 31st, 1878; in the County of Newberry, one mill for the payment of the funded indebtedness of said County, as provided for by Acts of the General Assembly, and one-half mill for past due school claims to be audited and funded; in the County of Oconee, one mill for past indebtedness of the County; in the County of Orangeburg, one-half of one mill, and no more, for past indebtedness of the County, as now provided by law; in the County of Spartanburg, one mill to retire railroad bonds, and one-half of one mill for past indebtedness; in the County of Sumter, one mill for past indebtedness; in the Counties of Williamsburg and Georgetown, one and one-half mills for past indebtedness. Sec. 3. That all the proceeds of the taxes levied for and on account of the State, as specified herein, shall be deposited and kept by the State Treasurer in such bank or banks or place of special deposit in the cities or towns of this State as in the judgment or discretion of the Financial Board of the State shall afford sufficient protection to the interests of the State; and the State Treasurer shall publish in one of the newspapers published in each of the cities of Columbia and Charleston a monthly statement of all the moneys received by him, and the amount paid out, and to whom, and on account of what appropriation paid, as well as the balance of moneys on hand. Sec. 4. That the County Auditors and County Treasurers of the several Counties of this State are hereby required, under the direction and supervision of the Comptroller General, to make the collection of the taxes levied under and pursuant to the provisions of this Act in the manner and at the time and under the conditions hereinafter to be provided; and they are hereby forbidden to collect any other tax except the taxes to meet the interest and retire the bonds issued by Counties in aid of railroads (and taxes to build fences under the statutes, and except also the special school tax authorized to be levied in School District No. 14, in the County of Fairfield,) whatsoever for the fiscal year, unless herein expressly authorized so to do; and any State or County officer who shall fail to comply with, or shall evade, or attempt to evade, the provisions of this Act shall he deemed guilty of a felony, and, upon conviction thereof, shall be punished by fine of not less than one thousand dollars nor more than five thousand dollars and by imprisonment in the Penitentiary for a period of not less than ohe year nor-more than five years. Sec. 5. There shall be assessed on all taxable polls in this State a tax of one dollar on each poll, the proceeds of which tax shall be applied solely to educational purposes. Every male citizen between the ages of twenty-one and sixty years, except those incapable of earning a support from being maimed, and except those who are now exempt by law, or from any other cause, shall be deemed taxable polls; and should any person fail or refuse to pay said poll tax, he shall be deemed guilty of a misdemeanor, and, on conviction thereof before any Trial Justice or other Court having jurisdiction of the same, shall be punished by a fine, which shall not exceed ten dollars, together with the cost of said suit, or by imprisonment in the County jail for a term not exceeding thirty days. In each school district of the County of Orangeburg having a school indebtedness past due there shall be levied and collected a special tax of one-half of one mill on the dollar of the value of the taxable property in such school district, if so much be necessary, to be applied to the payment of the past due indebteduess of the school districts wherein the same shall be collected. Sec. 6. That all taxes assessed and payable under this Act shall be paid in the following kinds of funds and no other: Gold and silver coin, United States currency and national bank notes; and for County taxes, jury and witness certificates in accordance with the Act passed at this session.
